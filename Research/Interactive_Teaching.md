**Study Summary: Interactive Teaching & Student Engagement**

The research examined how interactive teaching strategies and instructional perception (how students view the teaching) predict student engagement in a non-Western educational context.

**Key Findings**
- Multidimensional Engagement:
- Engagement is categorized into three types: Behavioral (participation), Emotional (enjoyment/belonging), and Cognitive (deep processing).  

- Predictors of Success:
- Cognitive Engagement is best driven by meaningful task design and participatory learning.  
- Emotional Engagement relies heavily on instructional perception, specifically goal clarity, inclusive environments, and timely feedback.  
- Behavioral Engagement requires a "dual-drive" of both effective interactive strategies and positive student perception.  
- Cultural Context: In non-Western settings, students may experience anxiety about public expression. Goal clarity and inclusive atmospheres are more effective for emotional engagement than Western-style student autonomy.  

- The Mediator: 
- "Instructional perception" acts as a bridge. If a student perceives a task as vague or the feedback as poor, even the best interactive strategy will fail to engage them.  

**Implementation in an AI Math Tutor**
To apply these empirical findings to an AI math tutoring system, you should focus on "perception optimization" and "culturally adaptive scaffolding".  

**- 1. Enhance Cognitive Engagement through "Meaningful Tasks"**
- Instead of just providing repetitive drills, the AI should use scenario-based simulations.  

- Application: 
- Use real-world math problems (e.g., calculating interest for a business or geometry for architecture) rather than abstract numbers.

- Interactive Design: 
- Implement real-time interaction, such as live polling or "step-by-step" collaborative problem solving where the AI and student co-construct the solution.  

**- 2. Boost Emotional Engagement through "Perceptual Clarity"**
- The study found that emotional investment is 61.9% dependent on how the student perceives the teaching quality.  

- Goal Transparency:
- Before starting a module, the AI should provide a clear "objective sheet".  

- Example:
- "This lesson on fractions will help you master measuring ingredients for recipes, which connects to your quiz next Friday."
- Inclusive Feedback: Provide timely and specific feedback. Instead of "Incorrect," use: "Your calculation of the area was thorough; next time, try checking your units first to ensure they match the question."  

**- 3. Drive Behavioral Participation via "Dual-Drive" Mechanisms**
- To prevent "pseudo-interaction" (where students just click through without learning), the AI must align its strategy with student needs.  

- Verification Steps: 
- Incorporate "mid-activity check-ins" to clarify doubts and ensure the student perceives the task as aligned with their learning goals.  

- Safety Net: 
- Create an inclusive environment by using encouraging language that reduces "face-saving" concerns or anxiety about making mistakes.  

**- 4. Cultural Adaptation:**
- If your users are in a non-Western or hierarchical educational context, prioritize explicit guidance over total student autonomy.  

- Structured Scaffolding: Provide clear hierarchical boundaries and expert-led paths rather than leaving the student to choose their own topics entirely, as this reduces anxiety and boosts the student's sense of competence.
